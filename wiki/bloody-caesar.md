# Bloody Caesar

**Background:** The Bloody Caesar was invented in 1969 by bartender Walter Chell at the Calgary Inn (now the Westin Hotel) in Calgary, Alberta, Canada, to celebrate the opening of Marco's Italian restaurant. Chell drew inspiration from the Venetian dish spaghetti alle vongole, combining vodka with Clamato (a blend of clam and tomato juices), Worcestershire, and hot sauce. It quickly became Canada's national cocktail — Canadians consume an estimated 400 million Caesars annually. The Clamato base is what distinguishes it from the American Bloody Mary. (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesar_(cocktail))

![](../photos/_TBD/pending-cocktails.jpg)

- **Glassware:** Collins
- **Ingredients:**
  - 1.5 oz Vodka
  - 3 oz Mott's Clamato Juice
  - 2 dashes Worcestershire Sauce
  - 2 dashes Tabasco Sauce
  - Juice of 1/4 lime
- **Instruction:** Rim the glass with lime juice, then dip in celery salt. Squeeze lime juice into the glass. Add ice, then add all remaining ingredients. Stir well to combine. Garnish with a celery stalk.
- **Garnish:** Celery salt rimmed glass and celery stalk
- **Profile:** Savoury, spicy, and tangy — like a Bloody Mary with added oceanic depth from the Clamato. The lime keeps it bright while Worcestershire and Tabasco build layered heat and umami.
