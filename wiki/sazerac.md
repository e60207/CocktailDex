# Sazerac

**Background:** The Sazerac is one of the oldest known American cocktails, originating in New Orleans in the mid-19th century. It evolved from a Cognac-based drink popularised by pharmacist Antoine Amédée Peychaud, who served his branded bitters with Sazerac-de-Forge et Fils Cognac in the 1850s. When phylloxera devastated French vineyards in the 1870s–1880s, rye whiskey replaced Cognac as the dominant spirit — though many modern recipes, including this one, use both. In 2008, the Sazerac was officially designated the cocktail of New Orleans. (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sazerac)

![](../photos/_TBD/pending-cocktails.jpg)

- **Glassware:** Rocks (small/espresso glass)
- **Ingredients:**
  - 1.5 oz Sazerac Straight Rye Whiskey
  - 1/2 oz Maison Rouge VSOP Cognac
  - 1/4 oz semi-rich simple syrup
  - 3 dashes Peychaud's Bitters
  - 1 dash Angostura Aromatic Bitters
  - Rinse of Absinthe
- **Instruction:** Add all ingredients into a mixing glass and stir to chill. Rinse the chilled glass with Absinthe. Pour drink into glass. Express oil from lemon peel and lay it across the glass.
- **Garnish:** Lemon peel
- **Profile:** Boldly boozy and slightly anise-forward from the absinthe rinse, with layered herbal bitterness from Peychaud's and Angostura, a whisper of sweetness, and a bright citrus lift from the expressed lemon peel.
