# Chicago Cocktail

**Background:** The Chicago Cocktail is a pre-Prohibition classic that first appeared in Robert Vermeire's *Cocktails How to Mix Them* (1922) and was later included in Harry Craddock's seminal *The Savoy Cocktail Book* (1930). It can be read as a Brandy Crusta in reverse — where most Champagne cocktails treat the sparkling wine as the star, the Chicago leads with Cognac and uses Champagne as a finishing lift, resulting in something closer to a sparkling brandy cocktail than a flute drink. The sugar-rimmed coupe and orange oil garnish give it a dressed-up elegance that has kept it on classic bar menus for over a century. (source: https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/16019/chicago)

![](../photos/_TBD/pending-cocktails.jpg)

- **Glassware:** Coupe
- **Ingredients:**
  - 1 oz Cognac
  - 1/4 oz Ferrand Dry Curaçao
  - 2 dashes Angostura bitters
  - 2 oz Brut Champagne (or dry sparkling wine)
  - Expressed orange oil
- **Instruction:** Prepare the coupe with a sugar rim. Add Cognac, Curaçao, and bitters into a shaking tin with ice. Shake to chill and strain into the prepared glass. Top with brut Champagne, then express orange oil over the surface.
- **Garnish:** Sugar rim.
- **Profile:** Boozy and refined — Cognac's warmth and Ferrand Dry Curaçao's orange notes are sharpened by Angostura bitters, then opened up with brut Champagne and finished with expressed orange oil against a sparkling sugar rim.
